The goal

Win chips by winning pots. You get two private hole cards. Five community cards land face-up on the table. Make your best five-card hand from any combination of the seven, or make everyone else fold.

Hand rankings (best to worst)

  1. Royal FlushA♠ K♠ Q♠ J♠ 10♠
  2. Straight Flush9♥ 8♥ 7♥ 6♥ 5♥
  3. Four of a KindQ Q Q Q 7
  4. Full HouseJ J J 4 4
  5. FlushA♣ J♣ 8♣ 6♣ 2♣
  6. Straight10 9 8 7 6
  7. Three of a Kind8 8 8 K 3
  8. Two PairK K 9 9 5
  9. One Pair10 10 A 7 2
  10. High CardA Q 9 5 3

Suits never break ties. An ace can be high (A-K-Q-J-10) or low (5-4-3-2-A).

Blinds & betting rounds

The dealer button rotates each hand. The two players to its left post forced bets: the small blind and big blind. Then four betting rounds happen:

  1. Preflop — after hole cards are dealt.
  2. Flop — after three community cards.
  3. Turn — after a fourth community card.
  4. River — after the fifth. Last bets, then showdown.

If betting makes everyone equal and more than one player remains, hands are compared at showdown and the best five-card hand takes the pot.

Your options on your turn

  • Fold — throw your hand away, lose no more chips.
  • Check — pass when nobody has bet.
  • Call — match the current bet.
  • Raise — increase it. Others must call, re-raise, or fold.
  • All-in — bet every chip. You can then win only the part of the pot you matched.

Quick math: if the pot is 100 and you must call 20, you need to win about 1 time in 6 to break even. That is called pot odds.

Beginner tips

  • Fold most hands before the flop. Two big cards or a pocket pair? Play on.
  • Acting last is an advantage — you see what everyone did first.
  • Matched bets after the river mean nothing if your hand cannot win. Bet for value or fold.
  • Suited connectors (7♥ 8♥) like cheap pots; big pairs like raising wars.
